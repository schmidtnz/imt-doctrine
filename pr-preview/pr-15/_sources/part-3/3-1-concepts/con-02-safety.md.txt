# CON-02 Risk management and safety

Safety is governed by the {{ org }} Safety Management System ({term}`SMS`), which covers training, exercises and operations, and meets both health and safety law and the memorandum of understanding with the Coordinating Authorities. Following it is mandatory for every member.

Safety management starts at the beginning of every operation and continues until everyone is back to normal business. Briefings happen at three levels: the incident controller or safety officer (strategic hazards and reporting), operations (task-specific hazards) and team leaders (how the team will manage risk).

## IMT obligations

1. Cover safety in every team briefing.
2. Assess and record operation-level hazards.
3. Prepare a safety plan, and use it to populate the hazard section of each tasking.
4. Monitor progress, update teams on changing hazards and look after their wellbeing.
5. Debrief teams on safety issues.
6. Record and report accidents and incidents.
7. Stay current with safety advisories and the {{ org }} safety standards.

Teams manage dynamic risk in the field with the Take 5 process. Groups benefit from an annual hazard register for their area, so briefings can focus on task-specific risks.

| Risk domain | Example controls |
|---|---|
| Operational environment | Weather and river thresholds, daylight limits, go/no-go decisions |
| Fatigue | Shift planning, rest periods, replacement teams, IMT fatigue monitoring |
| Comms and tracking | Scheduled calls, check-in/out, team tracking, lost team procedure |
| Psychological wellbeing | Pre-briefs for traumatic exposure, peer and post-incident support |
| Legal and ethical | Privacy, media discipline, scene preservation, safeguarding |
| Volunteer sustainability | Reasonable call-out expectations, employer support, avoiding burnout |
