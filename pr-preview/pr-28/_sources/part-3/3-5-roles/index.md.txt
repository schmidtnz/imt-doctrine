# 3.5 Roles and responsibilities

Who does what in relation to the doctrine.

| Actor | Role | Responsibilities for the doctrine |
|---|---|---|
| NZSAR Council | National strategic governance of the SAR system | Align sector guidelines with the doctrine; feed sector lessons into it |
| New Zealand Police | Coordinating Authority for Category I operations, including most land SAR | Endorse the doctrine; deploy {{ org }} often to build capability; contribute to processes and debriefs |
| RCCNZ | Coordinating Authority for Category II operations; directs a growing number of beacon-initiated land operations, usually through an on-scene coordinator | Endorse the doctrine; integrate land teams where needed |
| Police SAR squads | Local knowledge, subject information, joint training with groups | Contribute at all levels, especially processes and debriefs |
| {{ org }} national office | Custodian of the doctrine; supports the federation of volunteer groups | Maintain the doctrine; align training, systems and readiness reporting |
| Local {{ org }} groups | Volunteer response capability and local knowledge | Train in and apply the doctrine; maintain readiness to agreed standards |
| Iwi, whānau, community and land managers | Local knowledge, tikanga, access, family connection | Engage early and respectfully, especially where whenua, marae, wāhi tapu or private land are involved |

The doctrine is freely available to sector partners, and every organisation with relevant expertise is invited to contribute through the continuous improvement process.

:::{note}
IMT function roles (control, intelligence, planning, operations, logistics, safety, welfare, public information) are currently described in {doc}`/part-3/3-1-concepts/con-06-imt-cims`. Where they should sit is still under review.
:::
