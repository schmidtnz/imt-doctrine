# CON-04 Notification and awareness

Notification starts every operation. Reports usually reach a Coordinating Authority directly or through partners such as ambulance services or the Department of Conservation. All reports should be channelled to the Coordinating Authority, so urgency and response can be assessed with local knowledge.

Whoever receives and assesses the report becomes the **incident controller** and holds all {term}`CIMS` functions until they delegate them. Their first jobs are to record the key information (the Missing Person Report, {term}`MPR`, is a good guide) and to assess the likely scale and urgency.

- **Urgent and obvious**: deploy immediately using local knowledge and available assets.
- **Not obvious**: work through the first steps of the {doc}`initial response process <con-15-ir-process>` (MPR, map, information collection, obvious scenarios) before finalising search urgency, because urgency scored on thin information tends to overstate it.

Avoid planning alone. A {{ org }} IMT can be engaged early to support these decisions, and should always set up as if the operation will be protracted.

:::{note}
Whether notification is in scope for this doctrine is still to be confirmed against the doctrine brief.
:::
