# CON-14 The Bike Wheel model

The Bike Wheel is a visual model that turns lost person behaviour statistics into initial response objectives. It comes from international search theory and has been adapted in New Zealand to include management and intelligence objectives.

| Wheel element | Initial response objective |
|---|---|
| Hub | Process the planning point and search close in |
| Spokes | Search the intended route and travel corridors |
| Reflectors | Check hazards, attractants and areas with SAR history |
| Rim, with rocks at corridor ends | Contain the area at natural containment points |
| Stick in the wheel | Search areas suggested by early scenarios |
| Frame | Build the profile, establish comms, prepare for handover |

## Example initial response objectives (one operational period)

1. Complete the information collection plan and build the subject profile.
2. Establish reliable communications for the search area.
3. Contain the search area.
4. Process the planning point and complete a close-in search.
5. Search the area suggested by the initial scenario.
6. Search the intended route and check decision points.
7. Search travel corridors next to the intended route.
8. Check known hazards, attractants and SAR history locations.
9. Prepare the IAP for handover.

In extended search planning the same wheel is reused: what is left is "the spaces between the spokes", which are reduced, divided into regions and segments, and weighted.

:::{note}
Fixed distances shown in some versions of the figure, and attribution, are still being checked.
:::
