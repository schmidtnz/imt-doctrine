# Part 1 Foundational overview

## 1.1 Introduction

{{ org }} volunteers provide an independent, impartial and professional response in support of New Zealand's two Coordinating Authorities: New Zealand Police and the Rescue Coordination Centre New Zealand ({term}`RCCNZ`). When a Coordinating Authority engages {{ org }}, specialist Incident Management Teams ({term}`IMT`s) manage the operation. Volunteers also provide search and rescue capability during civil defence emergencies.

Land search management has developed unevenly, drawing on many government and non-government sources, so practice varies across the country. This doctrine sets out the principles, core concepts and proven methods that govern how IMTs organise, train and operate. It is the single source of truth for "how we do things".

The doctrine is:

- **Integrated**: it fits within the legislative and operational requirements of the Coordinating Authorities.
- **Authoritative, not prescriptive**: it gives a decision-making framework while preserving the flexibility IMTs need.
- **Based on proven practice**: it captures lessons from past operations and reviews.
- **Grounded in search theory**: local, national and international.
- **The foundation for consistency**: shared terms, processes and expectations so groups, Police and partners work together seamlessly.
- **Built for continuous learning**: one place to add, amend and improve.

## 1.2 Purpose

To set a unified, nationally consistent approach to IMT response processes and practices. The doctrine:

- draws on input from IMTs nationwide
- brings together national and regional coordinators, tutors, group IMT leaders and Police SAR coordinators
- pairs a single source of truth with a feedback loop, so operational learnings update the document
- embeds its principles in IMT training and operational evaluation
- makes it simple to benchmark against international practice.

## 1.3 Scope

The doctrine covers the delivery of effective land search and rescue within an operational management context. It cascades from principles to fundamental concepts, then to operational application, guidelines, and the templates, checklists and forms that follow from them. Training material is built on the same foundation.

It is not:

- a guide to every SAR sector function or agency activity
- a prescriptive, step-by-step operations manual
- a set of local standard operating procedures.

:::{note}
Scope is still to be settled against the doctrine brief, for example whether notification and Coordinating Authority processes are in or out.
:::

## 1.4 Assumptions and applicability

The doctrine consolidates current New Zealand practice from training, volunteer and Police operational experience, IMT tutors and national and international publications. It is deliberately not exhaustive.

It applies to all {{ org }} IMT personnel and assumes endorsement by Police and RCCNZ. It rests on a **federation model**:

| Who | Role |
|---|---|
| National {{ org }} | Strategy, standards, quality assurance, data systems, national partnerships |
| Local groups | Local identity and relationships, readiness, front-line response |
| Coordinating Authorities | Statutory control of the search and rescue operation ({term}`SAROP`) |

## 1.5 Audience

The primary audience is volunteer IMT members. The doctrine also gives Police, RCCNZ and partner organisations a transparent view of how {{ org }} manages operations, and other SAR organisations may use it as a reference.

## 1.6 Strategic context

New Zealand's terrain, weather, rivers, caves, alpine areas and visitor mobility demand local knowledge within a nationally coherent system. Coordination is split by jurisdiction:

| Category | Coordinated by | Typical operations |
|---|---|---|
| I | New Zealand Police (locally) | Land, subterranean, river, lake and close-to-shore |
| II | RCCNZ (nationally) | Missing aircraft, distress beacons, offshore marine |

The doctrine integrates established international search theory with local protocols and training. Joint Police, RCCNZ and {{ org }} training is the key to a consistent approach.

The **keep it simple** principle applies throughout. Groups with high operational tempo can go further with extended search planning and advanced theory, provided their insights come back into the national doctrine.
